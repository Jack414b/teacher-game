Markov Chains
Application: Language Modeling
Statistical language model: learn probability distribution of word sequences.
Applications:
▶ Sentence completion: predict the next word given the previous words in a
sentence.
▶ Data compression: use shorter codewords to represent more frequently used
strings to store the data.
▶ Text classification: classify the topic or meaning of a document.
State space: all words in a language, e.g., English.
Unigram model:p(x[t] =x)- marginal probability that a word isx.
Bigram model:p(x[t]|x[t−1])- probability that a word isx[t]depends on
immediate previous wordx[t−1].
n-gram model:p(x[t]|x[t−1], x[t−2], . . . , x[t−n+ 1])-x[t]depends on
n−1immediate previous words

Hidden Markov Models
Applications
Do not assume Markov property for the observationsx[0],x[1], . . .. Allows
long-range dependencies between observations captured through the latent
variablesz[0], z[1], . . .
Speech recognition:x[ t ]is the feature vector extracted from a speech signal
andz t is the word being spoken. The transition modelp(z[t]|z[t−1])
represents the language model, whilep(x[t]|z[t])represents the acoustic
model.
Activity recognition:x[t]is the feature vector extracted from an image or
video frame andz[t]is the type of activity a person is performing.
Gene finding:x[t]represents the DNA nucleotides (A, C, G, T) andz[t]
represents whether we are inside a gene-coding region or not.
Emission probabilities example: Ifz[t] =k, the observation
x[t]∼p(x[t]|ϕ k ) =N(x[t]|µ k ,Σ k ).
